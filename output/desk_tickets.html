101 — Customer order (Bulldog tee)

customer_order Tauhid Zaman CC-TEE-WHITE · S · qty 1 invoice_id 501

Expected

Boss calls first

Inventory. This is a size-specific order. Before anyone talks money or drafts a reply, we need the real on-hand count for CC-TEE-WHITE / S. If stock covers it, the path is short; if not, Inventory owns the shortfall and vendor question.

Delegations we expect (lean)

  • Boss → Inventory: “Can we fill ticket 101 from stock? If not, who would reprint and can they ship?”

Customer Service waits until Boss has a clear blocker/status to word.

Reflection — after resolving 101, 102, and 103

Written against this same page: Expected vs Actual on each ticket tab, and the Cash tab ($3,400 → $1,000 → $160). Human approver on both pays: Jake (human desk).

1. Human approvals (who actually moved cash)

Agents never silently paid. On 102, Accounting only prepare_payment_request’d rent for lease 1 at $2,400; Jake approved and execute_approved_payment dropped checking $3,400 → $1,000 (Cash tab). On 101, Accounting prepared invoice 501 / $840; Jake approved after rent, so checking went $1,000 → $160. On 103 there was no human pay — Boss’s discount call and a Customer Service draft — cash stayed $160. That split is the whole point of the desk: prepare in the agent lanes, cash-out only through the human approve control.

2. How I’d grade the agents on each ticket

Ticket 101 — Bulldog tee (Tauhid / CC-TEE-WHITE S)

Strong. Inventory told the truth: qty 0 for size S, so we never pretended we could pull a tee off Aisle B. The team correctly treated invoice 501 as the restock gate (Bulldog Print Co won’t ship with an open unpaid bill), left cash alone until Jake approved, then record_restock_plan only after the vendor cleared, and parked a draft for Tauhid on the board instead of emailing him. I’d dock a little for Boss talking to Customer Service and Accounting directly instead of the single Inventory→Accounting handoff we sketched — more chatter than needed — but the money and stock outcomes were right, and Facilities stayed out like it should on a tee order.

Ticket 102 — Rent due (Elm City / lease 1)

Strong on facts, messy on the second pass. Facilities nailed the lease against desk.date_today: $2,400, due 2026-09-02, not overdue on 2026-08-31. Accounting prepared rent without inventing an amount. Human pay worked — that’s the Cash tab’s first line. Inventory correctly never showed up. The awkward bit was after Jake’s pay: a follow-up turn briefly got confused about whether rent had cleared (agents tried to re-read cash and narrate a shortfall story even though payment id 1 already succeeded). We still ended resolved with the landlord draft on the ticket. Grade: A for the prepare→approve path, B− for post-pay narration noise.

Ticket 103 — Bulk hoodie discount (Yale AI Club / CC-HOOD-NAVY M × 20)

Best “judgment” ticket. Inventory’s 8-vs-20 shortfall is the whole plot; Accounting actually did the margin math at list $58 / cost $22 and at 10% off ($52.20, ~57.9% margin). Boss denied a deep bulk cut and offered at most 10% on the eight units in stock — which is a real shop answer, not “sure, discount twenty ghosts.” Customer Service’s draft stayed on the board. Cash Δ $0 on the Cash tab, which is correct: this wasn’t a pay ticket. Slight over-delegation (CS pinging Boss back; Boss re-asking Inventory) but the decision quality was high.

4. What would’ve been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 is the poster child for one agent. The path is almost a script: get_ticket → get_lease_details(1) → get_shop_today → get_cash_balance → prepare_payment_request(rent, 1, 2400) → wait for Jake → maybe append_ticket_notes. Splitting Facilities vs Accounting added latency and a second model call for a single lease row and one cash check. A solo “ops clerk” agent with the same MCP tools would’ve hit the same Cash tab result with less drama.

101 is where the team earns its keep — stock truth, vendor credit hold, invoice pay, then restock plan and a customer draft are different failure modes. One agent could still call the tools in order, but the prompts’ separated scopes (Inventory never invents a pay; Accounting never invents qty; CS never emails) are what kept Jake as the only person who could move the $840. 103 is judgment-heavy; one agent with get_stock_by_sku_size + get_sku_pricing could decide the 10% offer, but having Accounting forced to show margin before Boss spoke made the Yale AI Club answer feel less like a vibes discount.

5. Three new Campus Customs problems this team could solve with tools we already have

  1. Another size-stockout on an apparel SKU with a vendor already paid. Same pattern as 101 after invoice 501 cleared: Inventory get_stock_by_sku_size + vendor_can_accept_order + record_restock_plan, CS drafts the shopper note. No new tables required.
  2. Next month’s rent notice on lease 1. Clone of 102: Facilities get_lease_details, Accounting prepare_payment_request, human approve on the desk (Cash tab would show another −$2,400 if checking can cover it — today we’d refuse if balance stayed at $160).
  3. A second student-org bulk ask on a priced SKU (mug, hat, hoodie). Same as 103: stock vs qty, get_sku_pricing margin, Boss approve/deny/counter, CS board draft — cash stays put unless they also need a vendor reprint path like 101.

6. Three problems this team cannot solve yet — and what we’d add

  1. “Can we take a card deposit / recognize revenue when Tauhid picks up the tee?” This homework is cash-out only (Cash tab never goes up). We’d need a record_sale / receive_payment MCP tool writing a sales or deposits table, an Accounting skill for inbound cash, and probably a POS/agent that knows order fulfillment beyond record_restock_plan (which never bumps inventory.qty when goods arrive).
  2. “Ship the restocked tee to Tauhid’s dorm and email tracking.” CS is banned from real email; we have no carrier, address, or shipment table. We’d need a Logistics agent, tools like create_shipment / get_tracking, and a deliberate “send_message” channel that’s still sandbox-safe for class (or a mock mailer table).
  3. “Negotiate a lease amendment or new Chapel Street square footage.” Facilities can read lease 1’s rent and due date, but can’t model square footage, CAM charges, or amendment versions. We’d need richer lease documents (or a docs tool), a Facilities prompt for amendments, and probably Legal/Boss escalation that isn’t just update_ticket_status.

7. Closing note from the desk

The multi-agent board paid off most on 101 (stock + vendor credit + human $840) and 103 (margin before discount). 102 proved the human approve path on the Cash tab more than it proved five specialists. Ending checking $160 matching campus_customs_new.db after rent and invoice is the non-negotiable grade — the React “resolved” badges would be worthless if that math were wrong.
